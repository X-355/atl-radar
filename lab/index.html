1 · What big orders do around bottoms

A bottom is the lowest price within ±14 days, at least 30% below the prior 90-day high, followed by a 25% rally. Each bottom is compared with a falling knife on the same coin (also down 30%, but price kept falling at least another 10%) and with a random day. Every measure is shown relative to that coin's normal level. "Big orders" are taker orders of $100k or more.

4 · Paper bot: the Bitcoin 20-week guard

The one rule that passed a survivorship-free stress test. Hold Bitcoin while its weekly close is above its 20-week average; otherwise sit in USDT. Tested on Binance data from 2018 to October 2026, it cut Bitcoin's worst drop from −72% to −37% (+40% a year vs +34% for holding, at a cautious 0.2% fee per switch; the paper bot below pays 0.1%, also tested). From 2024 on it earned less than holding (+25% vs +35% a year) with a smaller worst drop (−34% vs −52%): crash insurance, not a profit booster. The same filter on baskets of top altcoins failed once delisted coins were included.

Below is a paper bot: every Monday after the weekly candle closes it logs what the rule would hold for the coming week, in a paper account that started at 1,000 USDT and pays 0.1% per switch. No exchange keys, no orders, no real money. Research, not financial advice.

5 · Polymarket 5-minute Bitcoin markets: are the prices fair?

Mostly yes, and the costs eat everything except one narrow window. Every 5 minutes Polymarket opens an "Up or Down" market on Bitcoin. We replayed the full order book of every well-covered day and asked three things: does the price match how often each side really wins, can a model forecast better than the price, and does any simple rule make money after the spread and Polymarket's taker fee? From 4 minutes left down to about 1 minute, the market's own price forecast the result at least as well as our models, the simple rules lost money after costs, and the models' few small wins were within noise. The exception is the last 30 seconds, where a gradient-tree model forecast better than the price and made money on both halves of the data. That zone is a speed race against bots, so treat it as a lead to paper-test, not a proven edge.

6 · Polybot: live paper test of the last-30-seconds lead

Section 5 found one lead: in the last 30 seconds of a 5-minute Bitcoin market, a gradient-tree model beat the market's price on both halves of the data. Polybot tests that live, with pretend money only. It runs around the clock on a small cloud server. 30 and 10 seconds before each window closes, it buys one side with a pretend $10, but only if the model's probability minus the price and Polymarket's fee is more than 2¢. Every trade is then scored against Polymarket's official result. The pass/fail rules were written down before the results came in, and the test can only pass if it beats simply buying the favourite.
